**Date**: 2026-10-09
**Status**: Decided

## Context

Every accepted edit becomes a commit in the studio's clone ([ADR-002](../adr-002/)). The clone's git identity is the service's (`STUDIO_GIT_AUTHOR_NAME`, `STUDIO_GIT_AUTHOR_EMAIL`). With people acting as themselves on both doors ([ADR-010](../adr-010/), [ADR-012](../adr-012/)), the history should say who made each change.

## Decision

The commit of a gated write names the acting person as its author (`--author "<name> <email>"`); the clone's configured identity, the service, stays its committer, and the service pushes. A write the service causes (the shared credential, the stand-alone Basic gate, a local run) names the service as author too. The git port takes the author on every commit, so no use case can commit anonymously.

## Rationale

Author and committer are git's own distinction between who wrote a change and who recorded it, which is exactly the split here: the person wrote it, the studio recorded and published it. Keeping one committer means the repository host's view of who pushed stays the deployment's credential.

## Consequences

- The repository host shows the service as the pusher; `git log` and blame show the person.
- A commit holds exactly its author's change because the corpus lock spans the whole write ([ADR-014](../adr-014/)).

## Alternatives considered and rejected

- **Commits as the person, pushed with their own credential**: the studio would hold every person's repository credential.
- **A `Co-authored-by` trailer**: the person is the author, not a co-author, and the org's commit hygiene refuses the trailer.
